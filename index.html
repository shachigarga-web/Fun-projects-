01 · How I measure

Three lenses, one answer

No single method tells the whole truth about media. I run models, experiments, and attribution together, and use each one to check the others. The goal is a number a CFO can defend and a media buyer can act on this week.

1 to 4x a year

Bayesian media mix modeling

Which channels should we be in, and at what spend level?

  • Recast, Triple Whale, Workmagic, BayesiaLab
  • Priors set before results are seen
  • Validated with recovery, holdout, and stability checks
4 to 12 week tests

Incrementality experiments

What did this channel, creative, or mix add on top of everything else?

  • Geo holdouts and matched markets
  • Difference-in-differences, synthetic control
  • Power and market matching settled up front
Weekly

Attribution and diagnostics

Which station, daypart, ad, or audience should we change right now?

  • Airing-level response windows for TV and radio
  • Deterministic ACR matching (iSpot), MMP data
  • Anomaly monitoring on CPL, CPM, CTR
Experiments calibrate the model The model sizes the budget Attribution steers the week

Set the priors before seeing results

Model configuration happens up front with the client. Tuning assumptions after the numbers come in is how an MMM quietly becomes biased.

Budget from the marginal number

Average CPA says what a channel has done. Marginal CPA says what the next dollar buys. Reallocation decisions always run on the second one.

Test before you reallocate

When a model's credible intervals overlap, I say so and design a holdout to settle it. A quarter spent testing beats a year spent on a wrong split.

Say what the data cannot prove

Correlation gets reported as correlation. Clients trust the measurement team more when it names its limits before anyone else does.

Case 01 · National home-services retailer · 8-figure annual media

A Bayesian MMM the client could trust, and the budget math that came out of it

RecastBayesian MMM12 channelsLeads KPI
Problem

A lead-gen advertiser spending across TV, CTV, radio, search, social, display, audio and direct mail was allocating budget on last-click and platform numbers, which over-credit branded search.

What I did

Built the daily mastersheet and prior sheet, split channels into demand creation and demand capture so brand search did not absorb TV's credit, and ran the full validation stack before anything reached the client: prior-predictive check, parameter recovery, holdout backtests, and a seven-week stability loop.

Insight

Every channel was past its efficient point. TV's next lead cost 1.8x its average, direct mail's cost 4.2x, while brand search stayed the cheapest place to buy the next lead. That gap reframed the budget conversation from "what worked" to "what the next dollar buys."

OutcomeA validated model the client acted on, plus the team SOP I wrote so every future Recast build clears the same thresholds.

Case 02 · Same client, 2026 refit · 152-day window

Telling a client what the model can and cannot support

Model diagnosticsCredible intervalsTest design
Problem

After a data rebuild, the client wanted the new dashboard to decide which channels to cut and which to fund. The front page showed CPAs to the dollar and invited a reallocation.

What I did

Read the diagnostics before the dashboard. The next lead cost 1.77x the average account-wide, but the 95% intervals overlapped heavily (TV anywhere from 79 to 893 leads, CTV 48 to 668), and two channels failed parameter recovery. I tiered each channel by how well the model actually measured it.

Call

The model supports sizing the total budget, and it does not yet support re-splitting it. Display was the one clear cut. For CTV, the strongest case on the page, I recommended a geo holdout to settle it inside a quarter.

Why it mattersA separate lead-impact read for the same client found blended leads up 17% on 29% more spend, with weekly spend and search leads correlated at only r = 0.38. I reported it as plausible but unproven and proposed a controlled market test.

How well the model learned each channel

Parameter recovery ratio (posterior error ÷ prior error). Lower is better.

Below 0.6 the channel is learning from data. Above 0.95 the model is mostly repeating the prior. Single run; 3 to 5 averaged runs before any channel is treated as settled. Brand search lines are declared lower-funnel and carry no truth line by design.

Case 03 · Education, home services, CTV programs

A geo-holdout playbook for when national media can't be paused

Geo holdoutMatched marketsDiff-in-diffThe Trade Desk
Problem

Clients running national TV wanted to know whether CTV and programmatic added anything on top. Platform lift studies and view-through numbers inflate that answer when national TV is live.

What I did

Wrote the house framework: national linear stays constant everywhere, the tested channel runs only in test DMAs, and 20 to 30% of matched markets are held out. Markets are matched on six to twelve months of history, and search, social, promos and landing pages are locked for the window.

In the field

Ran a state-level programmatic holdout for an online education provider across CTV, display, native, online video and streaming audio for three program lines. Set up a creative test for a flooring retailer with 38% of budget in test markets against controls and a pre-set target cost per lead.

OutcomeLift studies at p < 0.05 behind $MM+ cross-channel reallocation decisions, read with confidence intervals and pre-agreed decision rules.

Case 04 · National home services brand · DRTV + scatter

Finding where TV really converts: through search

Lift analysisFull-funnel KPIsEvent controls
Problem

After a government shutdown cut responses 45%, the client needed to know whether leaner DRTV budgets were still working and where to push next.

What I did

Read seven months of the funnel (Google Trends, branded and non-branded search, responses, leads, orders) against DRTV and scatter spend, with promotions and outside shocks marked so they were not mistaken for media effects.

Insight

Branded search rose 30% in Q1 with TV. The Hispanic-market DRTV line correlated far more with branded search (r ≈ 0.65) than with leads (r ≈ 0.30): those viewers are prompted by TV and convert through search. Orders tracked offers, so offers are the bottom-funnel lever.

OutcomeLeads +4% and orders +3% year over year on a leaner mix. The recommendation moved the plan from efficiency to demand, with a Q2 scatter read to isolate incremental impact.

Case 05 · Cross-advertiser creative book

Scoring creative before the money is spent

Meta-analysisAI classificationCreative fatigue
Problem

Creative calls were made after budget had already gone out, and tagging creative attributes by hand was slow and inconsistent across analysts.

What I did

Used AI classification to tag creative attributes across advertisers, ran a meta-analysis of creative and channel performance across the whole book, and turned it into a pre-flight scoring tool used in annual account planning.

Fatigue

For a flooring retailer, cumulative response rate fell to 1.0 to 1.4% as impressions kept climbing: extra weight was adding reach while response fell. Evergreen and laminate spots bought leads at a fraction of the cost of seasonal promo spots, often ten times cheaper. I paired this with a share-of-voice read showing a national competitor sharply ramping its TV spend.

OutcomeScores correlated with in-market ROAS at r = 0.78. Recommendations cut roughly 23% of wasted budget before it committed, and manual tagging dropped 17%.

What the scoring tool does

AI classifies each asset: format, hook, offer, talent, UGC vs. produced, length, CTA.

2
Learn

Meta-analysis across advertisers links attributes to in-market ROAS and CPL.

3
Score

New concepts get a pre-flight score before media is bought.

4
Watch

Response rate per impression by spot age flags fatigue and triggers rotation.

Case 06 · Consumer fintech app · Linear TV launch

Designing TV attribution for app installs

iSpotAppsFlyerMMM alignment
Problem

An app-first brand buying linear TV needed installs credited to TV without double-counting the social and digital activity running at the same time.

What I did

Designed a three-tier framework run in parallel: airing-level statistical attribution for direct-response networks, deterministic ACR matching for brand and prime, and direct-response codes as a hard check. Tight 5 to 10 minute windows limit false positives; 7 to 14 day comparisons of impulse vs. matchback calibrate the true TV effect.

Fit

Mapped every window and baseline choice to the client's existing Recast MMM so TV reads the same in both systems.

Three-tier TV attribution

1
Airing-level statistical attribution

MMP conversions matched to post-log timestamps by station and daypart, with lift over baseline. CPI by station, daypart, creative.

2
iSpot deterministic matching

ACR data from 14M+ smart TVs, household device graph to app events, 7 to 30 day lookback. Recommended when over 30% of budget is brand or prime.

3
Direct-response mechanisms

Channel-specific promo codes, QR to app store, vanity URLs for 1:1 checks.
